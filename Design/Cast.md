# GLOWFADE — Cast Bible

Every character, the show, the town and the building in this bible were designed for this game. None of them is based on an existing mascot or franchise, and none is meant to look like one.

## The show / setting

**The pitch.** On 21 December 1997, a live children's show staged as black-light theatre under a planetarium dome went dark for eight minutes. During those minutes its glowing costumes walked off the stage and up into the bleachers. You were seven, and you were sitting in those bleachers. Tonight is the solstice again, and you go back in. You carry a phosphor flashlight that leaves everything it touches glowing. You can freeze the costumed hosts in Curtain Call bows, and you end the night by running the planetarium's own sunrise.

**The show.** *Good Evening, Gloaming Glen!* (1989–1997) was a regional public-TV evening nature show for small children about "friends who live in the dark." It was staged as black-light theatre inside a converted planetarium and shot on 16 mm film, because the video cameras of the day smeared the glow. The puppeteers wore black velvet, the sets were matte black, and the six costumed hosts, called the Gloamies, glowed. Their fabric was dyed with Evergleam, an experimental phosphor from the sponsor, the Ostrander Dye Company. Evergleam soaks up light and keeps glowing after the lights go out.

**The Longest Night.** On 21 December 1997 the show aired a live winter-solstice special. Partway through, the broadcast went black for eight minutes. Viewers at home saw only the glowing costumes, which walked off the stage and up into the bleachers among the children. The crew swore every performer was already backstage. The show was pulled within a week.

**The building.** The Wexley Starhouse is a 1960s concrete planetarium in the lakeside town of Port Wexley. It became a TV studio in 1988.
- **Hub:** the domed Star Theatre, ringed by bleachers. A dead star projector stands in the middle like a giant insect, over the lift pit it was lowered into for servicing.
- **Wings:** six habitat sets built into the old exhibit halls:
  - Hesper's Lamp-Post Meadow
  - Peekins's Treetop Nursery
  - Pinny's Midnight Kitchen
  - Tapwell's Echo Lab (the old Sound and Hearing hall, full of whisper dishes)
  - Deepwick's flooded Deep-Dark Tank (the basement aquarium)
  - Digsley's Under-Stage Trap Room
- **In between:**
  - a costume workshop full of spare heads
  - black-velvet "void" corridors where your beam barely bounces
  - the projection booth, which is the safe room and save point

The power is dead, and the building's contents have already been sold. At sunrise a salvage crew empties the film vault and trucks every reel to a silver-reclamation plant, where the film is melted down for its silver. After tonight the footage will not exist anywhere.

**Why you're here.** You are a lost-media archivist who breaks in on the solstice to rescue 13 unaired master reels in 16 mm film canisters. Twelve are spread across the six wings. The 13th is locked in the booth's film vault. You also have a private reason. At seven you sat in those bleachers on the Longest Night. You remember the lights going out and nothing else, except that your hands glowed for a week afterwards. Over the night you learn two things:
- The dome speakers still play the Starhouse's old star-show narration even though the power is dead. A calm woman's voice describes the winter sky over Port Wexley. The narrator never speaks to you and never gives directions, but the clues add up:
  - She mentions "a small light moving in the west hall" when your flashlight is on there.
  - She hums the show's lullaby between constellations.
  - She goes silent whenever Hesper's eyespots are on screen.

  A player who pays attention works out that the narrator is Miss Hesper, speaking through the dome without a mouth. Every star she describes is a lamp she wants lit, and the last one is the star projector, the brightest lamp in Port Wexley.
- Reel 13 is an isolated booth-camera recording of the dark eight minutes. It shows one small child stand up in the bleachers and follow a warm, bobbing light down the steps into the projector pit beneath the dome. That child is you. The small glowing handprints the UV lens has shown you all night are yours, and under the lens your hands still glow the same way.

**Core loop.**
- **Chapters:** each chapter is one act of the show's running order. It is set in one wing and named after the segment that wing hosted. There is no clock on screen. The night ends when the dome's own dawn comes up.
- **Flashlight:** you have a battery-limited flashlight with an Evergleam twist. Anything the beam touches keeps glowing for about 8 seconds, including the Gloamies, and that is how you track them in the dark.
- **In each wing:**
  1. Find 2 reels, a fuse and batteries.
  2. Restore the wing's breaker. This is a loud, bright 10-second crescendo that alerts every active Gloamie.
  3. Carry the canisters to the booth and thread them on the projector.
- **Reels:** each reel you thread projects its footage onto the dome, opens the next wing and unlocks a tool.
- **Rules:** each Gloamie follows one readable rule, based on light, darkness, scent, hiding, false light or sound through the floor. Later acts pair opposite rules, so you are always deciding when to switch the light on.

**Tools (unlocked by reels).**
- **Glowsticks:** thrown light decoys that last 40 seconds.
- **Placeable lantern.**
- **Tin clickers:** thrown noisemakers.
- **UV lens:** it never counts as light to any Gloamie. It shows two things:
  - black-light paint
  - small, child-sized Evergleam handprints left on the Longest Night. They mark low shortcuts under sets and bleachers, and at the end they show the way into the projector pit.
- **Hatch wedges.**
- **Curtain Call fuses:** each wing's stage-manager desk has a black-light cue lamp. Firing it makes every Gloamie within 20 m stop on its nearest mark and take a bow for 5 seconds.

**Catch rule.** There is no gore and no death screen. A catch is a one-second jump scare, the screen floods with that Gloamie's glow colour, and you wake up in the projection booth. Any reels you were carrying drop where you were caught and glow so you can find them again. Pinny is the exception: she puts them on her kitchen shelf.

**Act by act.**

| Act | Wing | Active Gloamies | Its reels unlock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act I: Lamp-Lighting | Lamp-Post Meadow | Hesper | Glowsticks |
| Act II: Count-to-Ten | Treetop Nursery | Peekins, Hesper | Lantern |
| Act III: Snack-Time | Midnight Kitchen | Pinny, Peekins | Tin clickers, UV lens |
| Act IV: Tap-Along | Echo Lab | Tapwell, Pinny; Deepwick starts haunting one lamp per wing | Hatch wedges |
| Act V: Cue the Trap! | Under-Stage Trap Room | Digsley, Tapwell, Hesper | Curtain Call fuses |
| Act VI: Follow a Friendly Light | Deep-Dark Tank | Deepwick, Peekins | Reel 13 (opens the finale) |
| Finale: Sunrise Over the Glen | Star Theatre | All six | — |

**Finale: "Sunrise Over the Glen."**
1. You power the star projector and start its last program.
2. The program loads in six chimes, about 25 seconds apart. Each chime wakes one Gloamie on the stage around you.
3. A three-minute fake dawn creeps across the dome. As the light rises, Peekins stays curled where she is and Deepwick's lure stops fooling anyone. Each Gloamie curls up asleep on its mark once the dawn passes its threshold.
4. Hesper goes last. She abandons you for the projector lens and circles it until the sun is up.

**Shared systems (Unity).**
- **Animation:** all six Gloamies are Humanoid and share one Mixamo set (idle, walk, stalk, run, search, grab, catch), plus additive poses per character. A small spring/jiggle script drives the extra bones: antennae, ears, tails, wings, the lure rod and Pinny's snout.
- **AI:** each AI is a NavMeshAgent with an enum state machine. It reads these shared services: `LightRegistry`, `NoiseBus` (position + radius), `ScentTrail`, `HidingSpot`, `Hatch` (trapdoors and floor vents) and `LureNode`.
- **Glow shader:** one URP Shader Graph, where emission = mask × colour × charge. Charge rises while the flashlight cone hits the renderer and fades over about 8 seconds. It is set through a MaterialPropertyBlock.
- **Call-signs:** each Gloamie has a short toy-glockenspiel motif, taught by the "Who's Awake in the Glen?" poster in the lobby and the booth. The motif plays when the Gloamie starts hunting, and faintly while it idles within 15 m, so players can identify a threat by ear.
- **Player speeds:** crouch 1.3 m/s, walk 2.5 m/s, sprint 4.5 m/s.
- **Android:** add a gamma calibration screen. Matte-black corridors crush to pure black on many phones.

### Miss Hesper — "Lights low, voices soft."

**Look.**
- **Body:** a tall, slender silk moth in shaggy pale pistachio-green plush, with a thick cream fur ruff over the neck and shoulders.
- **Head:** a round fuzzy dome with two huge glossy black vinyl eyes. They have no pupils, so under your beam they show only two hard white highlights.
- **No mouth:** below the eyes is a smooth fur mound with a marker-drawn smile, mostly rubbed away.
- **Antennae:** two olive-gold fern-plume antennae, about 45 cm tall, with a few fronds snapped and hanging.
- **Wings:** a stiff felt cape hung from the shoulder blades to the backs of the knees, piped in plum velvet. It carries four mustard eyespots, two per side, whose clear vinyl centres look wet. Two frayed, dust-grey ribbon tails trail from the cape hem to the floor.
- **Costume details:** long white four-fingered opera gloves, and a tiny pair of felt hands appliquéd flat on her chest in a praying pose.
- **Wear:** the tops of the wings are sun-faded grey-green from the studio lamps, the felt has moth-eaten holes, the piping is peeling and the ruff is matted.
- *Glow fingerprint:* the four eyespot rings charge to a cold mint green, so in darkness you see four rings floating at shoulder height.

**Silhouette.** She is the tallest in the cast. A narrow body sits under two splayed fern plumes, and a long cape tapers into two dragging tails, like a feathered arrowhead. When she hunts, she shows you her back: the cape and its four rings come toward you, wings first. When she raises her arms the cape splits open into a huge triangle. That is her dive tell.

**On the show.** She was the soft-spoken hostess who opened every episode at her lamp-post: "Good evening, Glen-lings. Lights low, voices soft." She introduced each friend, explained the night sky and closed the show by dimming her lamp. She also read the star-show segments unseen, over the dome speakers. She "never needed a mouth, because Hesper listens."

**What went wrong.** She speaks on every reel anyway, muffled, from somewhere inside her chest. Crew logs say she "kept walking into the studio lamps" no matter who was in the suit, and that she "always seemed to be facing the wrong way." Her head eyes never move, but the wet vinyl centres of her eyespots swivel to follow you. The star-show narrator in the dome is her. She wants the star projector lit.

**In the game.** She punishes you for using the flashlight, but not the way a moth at a lamp does. Her face is a mask, her wings do the seeing, and her dust turns the whole cast on you.
- *Eyespots:* the four wet eyespot pupils are her real eyes, and they swivel to track you. Her sight cone points out of her back (200°, 12 m) and registers only lit lights. The UV lens never counts. When she hunts she turns her back and walks backwards, so the eyespots lead.
- *Dive:* if an eyespot sees your lit flashlight, her arms rise, the cape splits open and she flies straight at you at 6 m/s, faster than your sprint. Lighting her face does nothing by itself. Switch off the moment she turns her back on you.
- *Dusting:* her first contact claps the cape around you. Glowing green scales cover your own hands and sleeves for 20 seconds, which acts as the HUD. During that time every Gloamie receives your position every 4 seconds. A second contact while you are dusted is a catch.
- *Monitors (from Act V):* field monitors on rolling carts flicker on near you, showing her lamp-post set. Switch the monitor off within 4 seconds or she steps out of its glow.
- *Orbit:* at a decoy (glowstick, lantern or set lamp) she circles and bumps it for 15 seconds ("tik... tik-tik"), then knocks it dark.
- *Drift:* she wanders between lamp nodes at 1.5 m/s, humming.
- *Drawn:* every 0.5 seconds she scores each `LightRegistry` entry by intensity ÷ distance. Your flashlight counts only with line of sight within 25 m, and the UV lens never counts. She flutters to the winner at 3 m/s, wobbling side to side (a sine offset of ±1 m), and turns her back to it as she arrives.
- *Search:* when you switch the flashlight off, she loses you after 3 seconds. She circles your last light position for 10 seconds, then goes back to Drift.
- *Counterplay:*
  - Watch for her turning her back, then switch the light off and move.
  - Throw a glowstick or flip on a set lamp across the room, then slip past while she orbits it.
  - Navigate by UV lens.
  - Kill monitors fast.
  - Fire a Curtain Call.

**Sound.** A dry, papery flutter, like a moth trapped in a lampshade and recorded up close. A "tik... tik-tik" of her body hitting hot bulbs. The show's lullaby, hummed from inside her chest and, between constellations, from the dome speakers. Call-sign: three soft, falling celesta notes, the show's opening.

**Rig notes.** Humanoid.
- **Cape:** auto-rig will weight the cape's lower edge to the thighs and stretch it when she walks. In Blender, separate the cape and split it down the middle into WingL and WingR. Parent each half to UpperChest, or give each its own bone, so the halves can swing open on a dive.

- **Eyespot pupils:** four small dark discs parented to the wings, which LookAt the player in LateUpdate (clamped). The same four transforms are the origins of her rear sight cone.
- **Backward walk:** while she hunts, set the NavMeshAgent's `updateRotation` to false, face her away from her velocity and play the shared walk clip at speed −1.
- **Glow mask:** the four eyespot rings.

### Peekins — "Just one more peek!"

**Look.**
- **Body:** a dumpling-shaped little body in dense, short oatmeal-tan velour, pilled and matted.
- **Face:** a white blaze runs from crown to nose between two chocolate-brown "goggle" rings. Each ring frames a huge amber resin dome eye, 25 cm across, with a retroreflective backing. Under your beam the eyes flare gold like animal eyeshine; in darkness they stay dull.
- **Ears and back:** tiny round ears almost lost in the fur. A chocolate stripe down her spine hides a costume zipper stuck half-open on black velvet.
- **Limbs:** short, thick limbs and oversized grasping hands with pink rubber palms that grip like clamps. The thumbs are set slightly too far apart.
- **Clothing:** a faded powder-blue corduroy romper with mismatched buttons and a felt name patch reading PEEKINS.
- **Wear:** one eye dome is cracked in a spiderweb pattern, the romper knees are grey with floor dirt, and the fur on her palms and forearms is rubbed bald from years of clinging to the set's prop branches.
- *Glow fingerprint:* none. Only her twin gold eyeshine shows, and only for an instant when light first hits her.

**Silhouette.** She is the smallest shape in the building: a round ball on short legs with two disc eyes. When lit she snaps into an even smaller ball with her head tucked, or clamps herself around the nearest doorframe or railing like a loris on a branch. Her signature pose is both arms straight up over her head, the real loris defensive posture, which the show sold as "Ready or not!" It is the last thing you see.

**On the show.** She was the sleepy, curious baby of the Glen, who always wanted "just one more peek" at the night before bed. In "Count-to-Ten" she curled up in a ball and counted in a drowsy singsong. Then she unrolled with her arms raised: "Ready or not... here I POP!"

**What went wrong.** On every surviving reel she appears only in the dark segments. Whenever the stage lights come up, her branch is empty and still swaying. The performer sign-in sheet in the costume workshop has a name for every Gloamie except her: next to PEEKINS the line is blank. The suit is far too small for an adult.

**In the game.** She is a darkness creeper who punishes you for keeping the light off. Her rule is light and only light. You can stare straight at her in the dark and she keeps coming. Turning toward her changes nothing.
- *Lit test:* she counts as lit if any active light reaches her: inside the flashlight cone (angle check plus raycast), or within a lamp, lantern or glowstick radius with line of sight. The UV lens does not count.
- *Hide:* she waits at a hide node beside your route. A soft "flump" plays when she settles somewhere new behind you.
- *Creep:* in darkness she walks at 2.2 m/s and is silent on carpet. She prefers dark routes (raise the NavMesh area cost inside every active light's radius). She yawns her way through her count, "one... two...", louder as she gets closer.
- *Curl:* once lit, her eyeshine flares gold for an instant. Then she snaps into a tight ball, head tucked, hissing softly through the felt. Beside a doorframe, railing or table leg she clamps around it instead. Curled or clamped, she can't move.
- *Retreat:* if she stays lit for 8 seconds straight, she unrolls and scuttles out of the light at 3 m/s. She heads to the dark hide node farthest from that light and starts creeping again from a new direction. Pinning her with light only moves the threat.
- *Pop:* within 1 m she springs up with her arms raised. That is a catch.
- *Counterplay:*
  - Sweep corners with quick flashes instead of holding the beam.
  - Pin her briefly with the lantern or a set lamp while you work, knowing she will relocate and the light draws Hesper.
  - Drop a glowstick at her feet to send her scuttling off to another hide node.
  - Listen for the count, the hiss as she curls, and her rubber palms squeaking on hard floors and doorframes.
  - Keep your back to a wall.

**Sound.** A drowsy, yawning count through felt. A soft hiss as she curls up. Rubber palms squeaking on a doorframe. A faint creak from her resin eye domes. The "flump" of her settling somewhere out of sight. Call-sign: a two-note music-box yawn, a high note sliding down to a low one.

```text

T-pose, arms straight out, front-facing, symmetrical, neutral expression, game-ready, PBR textures, plain background.
```

**Meshy settings.** Pose: T-pose · Topology: Quad · Target polycount ~20k (she's small) · Symmetry: On · then Rig + export FBX.

**Rig notes.** Humanoid, and it works at child proportions.
- **Markers:** her legs are short, so place Mixamo's knee and groin markers by hand.
- **Eyes:** split the two eye domes into their own material. Their gold flare is an emission boost driven by the flashlight's angle, not a phosphor mask.
- **One-sided details:** paint the cracked dome and dirty knees into the texture after generation, because Symmetry On mirrors everything.
- **Curl and clamp poses:** author both as additive animations: spine and neck fully flexed with arms wrapped round the knees, and a side-hug around a vertical post. Raise the Spine and Neck muscle limits in the Avatar so the curl can close tightly.
- **Arms-up pose:** it can clip into her big head. Raise the Arm Up muscle limit in the Avatar, or author the pose as an additive animation.
- **Glow mask:** none.

### Auntie Pinny — "Not one crumb left behind!"

**Look.**
- **Colouring:** bold black-and-white tapir blocking. Her head, chest, belly, arms and legs are black short-pile velvet.
- **Saddle:** a wide cream saddle drapes over her back and hips and down both flanks, like a real tapir's. It is built as a quilted, button-tufted padded panel, and each tufted square is a stitched pocket.
- **Head:** long and low, thrust forward on a short neck, with tiny sunken button eyes and round ears piped in white felt.
- **Snout:** a short, wrinkled, flexible proboscis, about a hand and a half long, that overhangs her upper lip and curls at the tip. It is much shorter than any trunk. The black felt is worn shiny at the tip, where a soft pink rubber grip frames two nostrils.
- **Apron:** a small frilly buttermilk bib apron with tomato-red gingham trim sits on her chest. Its pocket holds a wooden spoon and a dented tin Crumb Jar.
- **Hands and feet:** black three-fingered mitten gloves and oversized hooves of hard grey rubber.
- **Wear:** the saddle is yellowed and mud-stained at the hem, its pockets bulge, the velvet is rubbed bald at knees and elbows down to grey backing mesh, and the apron is torn and crusted.
- *Glow fingerprint:* the quilted saddle charges to a pale blue-white. In darkness her black parts vanish and a glowing quilt floats by itself at hip height.

**Silhouette.** A deep barrel body carried high on long, thin, slightly knock-kneed legs, with a long, low head pushed forward and a short curling snout. One bright quilted saddle seems to drift on its own under a flashlight. This is real tapir camouflage, used as a scare.

**On the show.** She was the Glen's cozy midnight cook. She ran Snack-Time and Tidy-Up Time, plucked up every crumb one at a time with her clever snout and taught that a tidy nest is a happy nest.

**What went wrong.** The snout was never a puppet. It curls and grips in every take with nobody's hand inside it. The saddle is not padding either: every pocket is full. Tidy-Up Time got longer every season, and the Crumb Jar segment asked kids to mail in "anything you've lost." Her Midnight Kitchen is lined with those mailed-in things, arranged in neat rows on shelves: mittens, a retainer case, house keys, a library card. Each one has a handwritten label, and some labels are dated years after the show ended. Tonight she is tidying the building, and you are the last crumb.

**In the game.** She is a scent-trail juggernaut.
- *Trail:* the player drops a breadcrumb node every 0.5 m into a timestamped `Queue<Vector3>`. Nodes expire after 90 seconds.
- *Tidy:* with no trail to follow, she wanders her kitchen straightening the shelves.
- *Track:* once she sniffs a node within 4 m, she follows the trail node by node, skipping ahead to the freshest node inside her sniff radius. She starts at 2 m/s and speeds up on fresher nodes, to a maximum of 3.5 m/s. She never runs, and she never gives up while a trail exists.
- *Doors:* a closed door holds her for 4 seconds. Then she shoulders it off its hinges, and it stays open.
- *Pluck:* she tidies as she goes. Any loose prop within 3 m of her path, including your thrown glowsticks and tin clickers, gets plucked up with her snout and tucked into a saddle pocket. Decoys left on her trail don't last.
- *Snatch:* within 3 m and with line of sight, her snout whips out and catches your sleeve. You are slowed to 50% and tugged toward her for 2 seconds. If you drop the tool in your hand, she takes it instead and lets go, pausing 2 seconds to pocket it.
- *Calves:* striped plush tapir calves lie around her wing. Kick one and it squeaks, which pulls her straight to you.
- *Catch (1.2 m):* her snout curls around the camera with a long, wet snuffle that fades to white. Everything you were carrying turns up on her kitchen shelf, labelled with your name.
- *Counterplay:*
  - Break the trail by wading through water: mop buckets, the flooded tank, sprinkler test valves.
  - Hit an "air purge" switch to wipe every node in that zone. It recharges in 60 seconds.
  - Lead her round long loops while you grab objectives.
  - Sacrifice a glowstick or clicker to break a snatch.
  - Sprinting outpaces her, but from Act V the noise carries through the trapdoors to Digsley.

**Sound.** Wet, rhythmic snuffle-snorts. A thin, high tapir whistle when she picks up your trail. Keys and buttons clinking in her saddle pockets. Heavy, hollow hoof clops. Call-sign: a tin spoon tapping a jar four times, then a rising whistle.

### Dr. Tapwell — "Knock, knock... who's awake?"

**Look.**
- **Body:** gaunt and long-limbed, in shaggy charcoal fur shot with coarse silver-white guard hairs, like an old broom.
- **Face:** a pale grey, cracked-latex face with a pink-beige muzzle and a few wire whiskers.
- **Ears:** huge, thin, leathery cast-latex dishes, 55 cm tall, flesh-pink and grey with painted veins. When your beam hits them from behind, they glow translucent.
- **Eyes:** enormous, bulging amber-orange eyes with pinprick pupils, slightly wall-eyed.
- **Costume:** cracked half-moon spectacles on his snout, an ochre-mustard tweed waistcoat with missing buttons, a brass watch chain, and a broken brass miner's headlamp strapped between his ears.
- **Hands:** black gloves. The right middle finger is 40 cm long and skeletal: wire shrink-wrapped in dark latex, with knotty knuckles and split joints.
- **Tail:** a ragged, bushy tail drags behind him.
- **Wear:** the ears are torn and patched with silver gaffer tape, the fur is clumped with dust and the waistcoat is moth-eaten.
- *Glow fingerprint:* a phosphor ring around the tip of the long finger, added "so kids could follow it in the dark." In darkness you see one lilac-white dot drifting at head height, tapping.

**Silhouette.** A tall, stooped coat-hanger of a figure with two satellite-dish ears, his head cocked to listen and one absurdly long finger pointing ahead.

**On the show.** He was the night-time scientist who explained how creatures find their way in the dark, from the Echo Lab's whisper dishes. In "Tap-Along" he tapped a rhythm on the set for kids at home to copy on their bedroom walls.

**What went wrong.** Late reels show him tapping on the studio wall, pausing, and something inside the wall tapping back. On the show he pointed his long finger at the camera to pick "who's awake" for the next game. Tonight that finger comes looking for you inside your hiding spot.

**In the game.** He is a hiding-spot seeker who remembers where you hide and closes off your options. Hiding spots are lockers, costume racks, prop trunks, specimen cabinets and the space under desks.
- *Memory:* he keeps a first-in-first-out list of your last 7 hiding spots. Each memorised room gets a phosphor-chalk tally on its doorframe. The tallies are visible only under the UV lens, so you can check what he knows.
- *Warmth:* each use of a spot adds 1 warmth. At 3 he seals the spot with silver gaffer tape and a card reading "DO NOT DISTURB — EXPERIMENT IN PROGRESS", and it is gone for the night. The hiding map shrinks as the act goes on.
- *Patrol:* he moves at 1.8 m/s, visiting memorised spots first, then spots near your last noise.
- *Listen:* every few nodes he stops and swivels his ears for 2 seconds. Any `NoiseBus` event within 15 m sends him to the loudest one at 3 m/s.
- *Probe:* at each spot he knocks ("Knock, knock... who's awake?") and listens for 2 seconds. Then his long finger comes in through the spot's gaps: locker vents, trunk slats, between hanging costumes, under a desk's front panel. In the dark you see its lilac fingertip creeping toward you. Just before each probe he taps the panel it will come through, so spatial audio tells you where. Lean away (left, right or back; on Android, a swipe) before the fingertip reaches you.
  - How many probes: 2, plus 1 for each warmth the spot already has, minus 1 beside humming machinery, which hides the sound of you shifting.
  - Dodge every probe and he murmurs "...Hm. Nobody awake." and moves on, but the spot gains 1 extra warmth.
  - If the fingertip touches you, he whispers "Found you awake." That is a catch.
  - *Unity:* each `HidingSpot` has three lean anchors. Each probe targets one anchor, never the same one twice in a row, and the tap plays from that side 0.6 seconds before the finger enters.
- *Chase:* if he sees you in the open within 8 m, he chases at 4 m/s for up to 10 seconds. Then he checks the hiding spot nearest to where he lost you.
- *Stagger:* flash your beam into his eyes within 3 m to stun him for 2 seconds. This works once per encounter, and the flash draws Hesper.
- *Counterplay:*
  - Rotate hiding spots and check the UV tallies.
  - Throw tin clickers to pull him off course.
  - Pick roomy spots, which leave space to lean.
  - Listen for which panel he taps.

**Sound.** Dry knuckle taps on wood and metal ("tap... tap-tap... tap"), then total silence while he listens. The leathery creak of his ears swivelling toward you. The dry whisper of the long finger sliding through a gap. Call-sign: the Tap-Along tune played on a woodblock.

### Mrs. Deepwick — "Follow a friendly light."

**Look.**
- **Head:** big and round, in glossy wet-look navy-black rubber over cracked fiberglass. It peels in patches to show yellowed fiberglass and mesh.
- **Jaw:** a wide underbite, closed and wound in a long sea-glass-teal knitted scarf like a mouth-cozy. A single row of short, blunt white felt teeth shows above the knit.
- **Eyes:** small, milky-white resin eyes set high on the head.
- **Lure:** a flexible rod of fiberglass in cracked rubber, like a fishing rod, arcs forward and down from her brow. It ends in a fist-sized frosted bulb with a warm amber light inside.
- **Markings and fins:** a row of cyan dots along each side of the jaw and down her flanks, ragged rubber fins on her shoulders, and a soft spine ridge down the back of her head.
- **Cardigan:** chunky, stretched-out sea-glass-teal cable-knit, unravelling at the hem. One pocket holds knitting needles and a trailing ball of yarn. Two tiny closed-eyed fish faces are stitched onto her side like patches.
- **Feet:** heavy brass-toed diver's boots.
- *Glow fingerprint:* the amber lure is a real light, and the cyan dots charge under your beam until they outline her jaw.

**Silhouette.** A question mark: an enormous head on a small body, with a thin arcing rod and one bobbing light. In darkness the only thing you see is a soft, warm light hovering at about 1.7 m.

**On the show.** She was the Glen's grandmotherly deep-sea explorer, who "brings her own light to the darkest place of all." She knitted, told stories from the bottom of the sea and sang the show's famous safety song: "If you're lost in the dark, follow a friendly light."

**What went wrong.** Real anglerfish catch prey with their light, and this show taught a generation of kids to follow one. The two faces on her cardigan were her "Tagalong Twins," who "followed Mrs. Deepwick's light and never left her side." Reel 13 shows that the warm light seven-year-old you followed down into the projector pit was hers.

**In the game.** She is a false-light ambusher with two modes. She poisons your safe spots: the warm amber wall lamps where you recharge the flashlight.
- *Lure:* she stands motionless at a lure node in a dark room or dead end, her body in shadow and only the bulb lit, posing as a recharge lamp. Step within 2 m and she lunges in 0.5 seconds. That is a catch.
- *Stalk:* with the lure off, she follows you in the dark at 2.4 m/s. Her diver's boots are loud and can be heard from 15 m. When she is out of your view, she cuts ahead to a lure node on your likely route and switches back to Lure.
- *From Act IV:* one recharge lamp in each wing is secretly her. She becomes fully active in Act VI.
- *Tells:* real lamps are wall-mounted on a bracket at 2.2 m, steady and silent. Her bulb hangs at 1.7 m, sways like a slow pendulum (±10°) and hums.
  - Sweep your beam over a suspicious lamp to charge her cyan dots and reveal the jaw.
  - Or throw something at it. If it's her, she snaps at the object, gives herself away and switches to Stalk, which gives you a head start.
- *Catch:* the bulb swings into your face in a white-amber flash, and the scarf begins to unwind.
- *Unity:* her lure reuses the recharge-lamp bulb mesh and light. Switch her to chase when the player gets close, or once her flashlight charge passes a threshold.

**Sound.** A low 60 Hz electric hum with the tink of a hot filament. A slow, wet creak as the rod sways. Brass-boot clomps and clicking knitting needles in Stalk mode. Call-sign: "Follow a Friendly Light" played slightly too slowly on a music box, coming from the bulb.

### Digsley Burrowes — "Standby... and GO!"

**Look.**
- **Body:** a stout barrel in deep chocolate-black crushed velvet that throws silvery highlights under your beam.
- **Face:** a star nose instead of a face: a ring of 22 glossy pink silicone tentacles, about 15 cm each, around the nostrils. A few are torn to stubs, and they never stop moving.
- **Goggles:** round crew goggles with big cheerful cartoon eyes painted on the lenses. He is blind; behind the goggles are only two pinhole beads in the fur.
- **Paws:** enormous spades, each the size of a shovel blade, with pink rubber palms and five thick yellowed claws. Fingerless leather rigging gloves are strapped across the palms. The paws are permanently turned outward at the wrist in a way that looks wrong.
- **Costume:** a faded crimson stage-crew cap worn backwards, with a clunky black headset clamped over it and its boom mic snapped off. A crimson canvas stagehand's tool apron over a black work shirt, its pockets stuffed with gaffer rolls, a trap-winch crank handle and a coil of sash cord. Chalk-white cue numbers are stencilled across the apron.
- **Tail:** short, thick, pink and scaly.
- **Wear:** the velvet is crushed bald in patches, the apron is stiff with sawdust and stage paint, and the cap has faded to a dusty rose.
- *Glow fingerprint:* the star nose and the painted goggle eyes charge to pink. So do X-shaped strips of glow tape on his apron bib, the spike marks stage crews use to find their places in the dark. In darkness a pink ring of points with two cartoon eyes hangs above two glowing X's.

**Silhouette.** Wide and low, as wide as he is tall to the shoulder. Two huge, outturned shovel hands, a backwards cap with a headset hoop, and a ring of tentacles where a face should be.

**On the show.** He was the Starhouse's cheerful trap-room operator, who "doesn't need eyes to see!" and ran the stage's real trapdoors from underneath. In "Cue the Trap!" he popped up through them at the wrong moment. Hesper tapped the trapdoor shut with her foot ("Not now, Digsley!") and the kids at home stomped along.

**What went wrong.** Some of the trapdoors under the Starhouse appear on no blueprint, and his trapdoors now open in rooms with concrete floors. Every trapdoor's red cue lamp still works, though the power is dead. On the reels, his star nose swings toward the camera the moment a child in the audience stands up. On the Longest Night, one child did.

**In the game.** He is a trapdoor ambusher who hunts by sound leaking through the gaps in the floor. He is active from Act V.
- *Under the stage:* his renderer is off. He travels a hidden graph of `Hatch` nodes (trapdoors, floor vents and lift panels) at 4 m/s. You track him by the trap room's machinery: winch chains clank as he passes, the cue lamps along the floor blink in sequence, and a pink tentacle tip feels up through a vent. His muffled whistling of the show theme stops when he locks on to you.
- *Gaps:* he hears only through gaps. A noise reaches him only if its radius touches an open trapdoor or floor vent:

  | Noise | Radius |
  |---|---|
  | Sprint | 15 m |
  | Walk | 6 m |
  | Landing from a jump | 10 m |
  | Thrown object (where it lands) | 8 m |

  Crouch-walking and standing still make no noise. A noise with no gap in range never reaches him.
- *Standby (1.5 s):* when a noise locks your position, the red cue lamp beside the nearest trapdoor lights and a stage bell dings once.
- *Go:* the trapdoor bangs open, and he erupts and grabs everything within 1.8 m. That is a catch. If he misses, he searches above ground for 4 seconds at 2 m/s, then drops back through the nearest open trapdoor.
- *BONK:* during Standby, stand on that trapdoor and press interact to stomp it shut. He is stunned and gone for 20 seconds.
- *Wedges:* a hatch wedge jams a trapdoor or vent shut for good and seals its gap, so it no longer carries sound. Use wedges to build quiet zones before long interactions.
- *No-trap ground:* set platforms, tables, the bleachers and any floor with no trapdoor or vent in range are out of his reach. He can't hear you there and has nowhere to come up.
- *Counterplay:*
  - Crouch or freeze when the cue lamps start blinking.
  - Throw tin clickers onto a far trapdoor to send him there.
  - Remember that sprinting is loud, so running from Pinny gets dangerous.

**Sound.** The clank of trap-winch chains and the rattle of pulleys under the floor. Cheerful, muffled whistling from below. A single stage-bell ding on Standby. A wet flicker of tentacles when he surfaces. Call-sign: three rising xylophone notes, the show's trapdoor "pop" sting.

## Making the models

1. **Generate.**
   - In Meshy, open Text to 3D and paste the character's prompt.
   - Set Pose: **T-pose**, Topology: **Quad**, Target polycount **~30k** (Peekins ~20k) and Symmetry: **On**.
   - From the previews, pick the one with the arms clearly separate from the body, two arms, two legs and a head, and nothing fused to the limbs. Check the side view.
2. **Texture.**
   - Run Texture with PBR maps on, reusing the prompt's colour words.
   - Paint one-sided details afterwards, because symmetry mirrors them (Peekins's cracked eye, Deepwick's twin patches).
   - Paint a black-and-white glow mask over the base colour (white = phosphor; each character's Rig notes list the areas) and save it as `<Name>_Glow.png`.
3. **Prep in Blender, only where the Rig notes say.**
   - Split or remove the parts that confuse auto-rigging: Hesper's cape, Tapwell's finger and tail, and any lure rod Meshy adds to Deepwick.
   - Add Pinny's 3-bone snout chain.
   - Apply transforms, then re-export as FBX.
4. **Auto-rig.**
   - Use Meshy Rig (humanoid), or upload the FBX to Mixamo.
   - Place the chin, wrist, elbow, knee and groin markers by hand.
   - Choose the no-fingers skeleton for Pinny, Deepwick and Digsley.
   - Preview a walk and look for stretching on capes, ears and big heads.
5. **Export.**
   - Export FBX (binary, with skin, in T-pose) plus the texture maps: base colour, normal, metallic/roughness and the glow mask.
   - From Mixamo, download the shared clips once (idle, walk, stalk, run, search, grab) as "FBX for Unity", without skin.
6. **Place the files** in `D:\Buddy\Assets\Characters\<Name>\`, using these folder names: `MissHesper`, `Peekins`, `AuntiePinny`, `DrTapwell`, `MrsDeepwick`, `Digsley`. Put the FBX in the root and the maps in `Textures\`.
7. **Import into Unity.**
   1. Select the FBX. On the Rig tab, set Animation Type: **Humanoid** and Avatar Definition: **Create From This Model**, then Apply.
   2. Click Configure. Confirm that every required bone is mapped, and use Enforce T-Pose.
   3. On the Model tab, set the Scale Factor so the height matches the cast table.
   4. Leave **Optimize Game Objects** off, so the extra bones (wings, ears, tails, rod, snout) stay reachable for the spring script.
   5. Extract the materials and switch them to the URP phosphor shader with the glow mask.
   6. Set the shared Mixamo clips to Humanoid, copying the avatar from any one character.
8. **Mobile pass.** For Android and WebGL, add an LOD1 at about 12k triangles with a Unity LOD Group, and use 1K textures.

## Originality notes

- **Miss Hesper:** a mouthless pale-green silk-moth hostess with a pistachio-and-plum palette. No listed franchise has a moth mascot. What sets her apart is what only she does:
  - She sees through her wing eyespots, whose pupils track you.
  - She stalks you backwards, wings first, and dives when her back catches your light.
  - Her Dusting marks you for every Gloamie.
  - She steps out of field monitors.
  - She orbits your decoys and knocks them dark.

  Trailers and store text should lead with those traits, never with "a moth that hunts your flashlight." Her narration is ambient star-show script that players decode from clues: it reacts to your light, hums her lullaby and goes quiet when her eyespots are on screen. It never addresses or guides the player, so there is no friendly helper who turns out to be the monster.
- **Peekins:** slow lorises are unused in mascot horror. Her rule is purely light-based. Being looked at or turned toward does nothing to her. When lit she curls into a ball or clamps onto a doorframe, which are real loris defences, and after a few seconds she scuttles off to a new hiding place instead of holding still like a statue. She never covers her face or eyes. She is sleepy and curious rather than shy, wears a powder-blue corduroy romper, and glows only with eyeshine. Her name and catchphrase avoid "blink" and "boo."
- **Auntie Pinny:**
  - She is a black-and-white Malayan tapir cook with a long, low head, a barrel body on long thin legs, and a cream saddle across her back and flanks rather than a cream belly.
  - Her name is built on her apron ("pinny"), with no "Snor-" prefix.
  - She collects with a short, prehensile tapir snout that plucks and snatches, and stores things in her quilt pockets. There is no vacuum, hose or suction.
  - Her snout has no trunk length and she has no tusks, so she is clearly not an elephant or a pig.
  - Her scares are the floating-saddle camouflage and a scent-trail AI, and she skips the folklore "dream-eater" angle.
- **Dr. Tapwell:** an aye-aye scientist in ochre tweed, with dish ears and one long finger, based in an acoustics lab rather than a library. No aye-aye mascot exists. His hiding check is a spatial dodge with no rhythm or timing test: he taps the panel he's about to probe, and you lean away from his glowing fingertip. His sealed-spot cards come from his own "experiment" persona.
- **Mrs. Deepwick:** a grandmotherly anglerfish in a cardigan and diver's boots. Her jaw is closed and wrapped in a scarf, with blunt felt teeth instead of a toothy grin. Her name avoids "Granny," and her fake-recharge-lamp ambush belongs only to this game.
- **Digsley Burrowes:** a blind star-nosed mole who works as a theatre stage-trap operator. He wears a backwards crimson crew cap, a headset and a tool apron, has painted cartoon goggle eyes, and glows through stage spike tape. There is no hard hat, overalls, miner palette or mining, and his name doesn't spell out the star nose. He hunts by sound leaking through trapdoor gaps and vents, not ground vibration. Stage cue lamps and a bell telegraph his attacks, and the counter is stomping the trapdoor shut or wedging the gap.
- **World:**
  - Gloaming Glen, the Gloamies, Evergleam, the Ostrander Dye Company, the Wexley Starhouse and Port Wexley are all invented.
  - No listed franchise uses black-light theatre in a planetarium.
  - Pitches lead with what only this game has: the eight-minute solstice blackout with glowing costumes walking into the bleachers, black-light theatre under a dome, the Evergleam flashlight, Curtain Call bows and the projector dawn.
  - The night's deadline is a salvage pickup that will melt the reels for their silver, not a demolition.
  - Chapters are named for show segments, not clock hours.
  - The lore comes on 16 mm film projected onto the dome, not tapes.
  - The protagonist's childhood link is playable: their own glowing handprints under the UV lens mark shortcuts and the final route.
  - On the Longest Night, the lure leads a child into the projector pit, not backstage.
